How this ranking is scored

We kept zero-ozone ENERGY STAR air purifiers certified for rooms of 250 sq ft or less and compared those with the highest smoke CADR, weighing smoke CADR 80% and the five-year electricity cost 20% (cost is scored against the page's cheapest unit; a heavier cost weight would put the cheapest-to-run unit first even when it cleans far slower) (certified yearly energy use x current EIA U.S. average residential price x 5).

  • Smoke CADR (80%): how fast the unit removes smoke-sized particles, the smallest of the three certified test particles (higher is better)
  • 5-year electricity cost (20%): the certified yearly energy use priced at the current U.S. average residential electricity rate (EIA) over five years; the certified yearly energy use assumes about 16 hours of running a day (the certified records imply a median of 5,904 hours a year), so running it around the clock costs about 1.5 times the figure shown (lower is better) Computed: certified kWh per year x EIA U.S. average residential price x 5.
  • Rated room size (0%): shown, not scored: the largest room ENERGY STAR certifies it for, derived from smoke CADR (already scored), so scoring both would count cleaning speed twice; check it against your room (higher is better)

Each number is scored against the best on this page (best = 100%; e.g. 90% of the best capacity = 0.9) and multiplied by its weight; the total is out of 100. Full method.

FAQ

How much cleaning power do small-room models have?

Certified smoke CADR on this page runs from 136 to 161 cfm.

What does it cost to run one of these?

At the U.S. average residential electricity price of 0.1816 dollars per kWh (EIA, 2026-08), the certified yearly energy use of the models on this page costs 92 to 147 US dollars over five years. Your state's rate may be higher or lower.

Do any of these produce ozone?

Each reports 0 ppb ozone in its ENERGY STAR certification, as its maker reported it. UL's zero-ozone test (UL 2998) cannot quantify a reading below 5 ppb, so 0 ppb and the small non-zero reports some other models carry are not reliably different; keeping only 0 ppb reports is our conservative choice, and the units it leaves out that would score highest are named under the table (see also our air purifier ozone and room-fit page).

Where do these numbers come from?

CADR, room size and energy use come from the U.S. EPA ENERGY STAR certified Room Air Cleaners dataset (retrieved 2026-11-01); the electricity price comes from the U.S. EIA Electric Power Monthly (2026-08). We did not test the units ourselves; we rank the certified results and compute the running cost.
