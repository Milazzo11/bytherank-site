Air purifier ozone and room fit, from federal certification data

Updated 2026-11-01 · Next refresh 2026-12-01

38 of 164 ENERGY STAR certified air purifier spec sets report ozone above 0 ppb, up to 5 ppb, none above the 5 ppb quantifiable limit of UL's zero-ozone test (UL 2998), so ozone does not separate certified models; room fit does: 82 spec sets alone reach 5 air changes an hour in a 250 sq ft room with an 8 ft ceiling. UL 2998 cannot quantify a reading below 5 ppb, and a report of exactly 5 ppb (the highest here: Blueair Pure 211+) sits at the lowest level it can quantify, not above it. Certification allows at most 50 ppb. The 250 sq ft room (8 ft ceiling) needs 167 cfm of rated smoke CADR; 138 spec sets reach it with up to two units (ENERGY STAR list only). Source: ENERGY STAR certified Room Air Cleaners dataset (U.S. EPA, data.energystar.gov gaa3-swy6), data retrieved 2026-11-01.

Read this first
  • Ozone figures are what each partner reported in federal certification data (UL 867 Ed. 5.0), dated 2026-11-01. ENERGY STAR certification allows at most 50 ppb, so every value here is far below that limit. Values are mostly whole numbers of ppb, so this page counts models above 0 ppb and does not rank them by ozone. UL's zero-ozone validation (UL 2998) treats 5 ppb as the quantifiable limit of detection, so reported values below it cannot be told apart reliably.
  • The technology column is the partner's own description. Some listed only as fan and filter report ozone above 0 ppb; the data does not say why, and neither do we.
  • CADR is measured at maximum speed (DOE test procedure, 10 CFR 430 Subpart B Appendix FF, which uses the AHAM AC-1 test). At lower speeds a purifier delivers less clean air, so a room reaching the target on paper may not reach it at the speed you run.
  • The 5 air changes per hour target is CDC/NIOSH workplace guidance: aim for 5 or more air changes per hour of clean air "to help reduce the amount of viral particles in the air"; CDC calls it "a rough guide", not a hard-and-fast rule (CDC page published 2024-10-15, updated 2026-10-28, checked 2026-11-03). The room math assumes well-mixed air and counts only the purifiers, not the room's own ventilation.
  • Yearly energy use in certification is calculated as DOE's test procedure sets out (Appendix FF, section 7), not 24/7 at full speed, so this page does not combine it with the room-fit numbers into a running cost. Noise is not in the data.

Method

We read the U.S. EPA ENERGY STAR certified room air cleaners list, grouped listings with identical certified values on every reported field into spec sets (so a model sold under several brands counts once), and computed each table from that list at build time. Ozone is the reported ppb field; room fit divides each set's rated smoke CADR into the airflow a named room needs at the target. We did not run our own tests. Counts are per certified spec set: listings with identical certified values on every reported field count once (247 listings, 191 models, 164 spec sets). Every number is computed from the certified list when the page is built, except 3 cited constants, the ENERGY STAR ozone limit (ppb, UL 867 Ed. 5.0) = 50, the CDC/NIOSH 'Aim for 5' air changes per hour of clean air = 5 and the UL 2998 quantifiable limit of detection for ozone (ppb) = 5, each sourced and dated under Sources; and the sold-as names on the per-set table, sourced in the note above that table. FAQ

Does an air purifier marked fan and filter emit no ozone?

The data cannot settle it. Some listed only as fan and filter report ozone above 0 ppb (see the table), but the highest value any certified model reports is 5 ppb, and UL's zero-ozone test (UL 2998) treats 5 ppb as its quantifiable limit. Certification allows 50 ppb.

How many air purifiers do I need for 1,000 sq ft?

At 5 air changes per hour with an 8 ft ceiling you need 667 cfm of rated smoke CADR. 0 certified spec sets reach it alone, 31 with up to two units and 70 with up to three.

Where do these numbers come from?

The U.S. EPA ENERGY STAR certified room air cleaners dataset (retrieved 2026-11-01), the ENERGY STAR ozone limit, UL's ozone limit of detection and CDC/NIOSH's air-change guidance. We compute the counts; the next refresh is due 2026-12-01.
