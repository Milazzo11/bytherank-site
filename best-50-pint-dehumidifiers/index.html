How this ranking is scored

We took portable ENERGY STAR dehumidifiers certified at 45 to 55 pints per day (the 50-pint class under the current DOE test) and compared the most efficient by IEF, with a 10% weight on capacity. The five-year electricity cost is shown for each (certified yearly energy use x current EIA U.S. average residential price x 5). Every model tied with the last one on the selection criterion is listed, so the cut never picks among equals.

  • Integrated Energy Factor (90%): liters of water removed per kWh including standby power (U.S. DOE test): the efficiency number (higher is better)
  • Certified capacity (10%): water removed per day under the current DOE test (higher is better)
  • 5-year electricity cost (0%): shown, not scored (it follows from capacity and IEF, already scored): the certified yearly energy use priced at the current U.S. average residential rate (EIA) over five years (lower is better) Computed: certified kWh per year x EIA U.S. average residential price x 5.

Each number is scored against the best on this page (best = 100%; e.g. 90% of the best capacity = 0.9) and multiplied by its weight; the total is out of 100. Full method.

FAQ

Why do some '50-pint' units show fewer pints than the box?

The U.S. DOE test procedure for dehumidifiers (Appendix X1) uses cooler, more realistic conditions than the older test, so many units marketed under an older, larger rating certify lower. This page uses the current certified figure.

What is IEF?

Integrated Energy Factor: liters of water removed per kilowatt-hour, including standby power, under the U.S. DOE test procedure. Here it runs from 2.02 to 2.24 L/kWh.

What does it cost to run one of these?

At the U.S. average residential electricity price of 0.1816 dollars per kWh (EIA, 2026-08), the certified yearly energy use of the models on this page costs 403 to 481 US dollars over five years. Your state's rate and how many months it runs change this.

Where do these numbers come from?

Capacity, IEF and yearly energy use come from the U.S. EPA ENERGY STAR certified Dehumidifiers dataset (retrieved 2026-11-01); the electricity price from the U.S. EIA Electric Power Monthly (2026-08). We rank the certified results and compute the running cost; we did not run our own tests.
