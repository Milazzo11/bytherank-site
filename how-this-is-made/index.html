How this is made

In short: ByTheRank's research system was designed by our editor, Maximus Milazzo, together with AI agents, and is run by a team of AI research agents (Anthropic's Claude) plus automated software. Here is what it does:

  • What we collect. Official test results: the figures every certified product must report to the U.S. EPA's ENERGY STAR program, measured under U.S. government or industry test procedures, plus official U.S. electricity prices. No customer reviews, no paid placements, and no hands-on tests of our own.
  • How we score. Each page says which numbers matter for its question and how much each counts. Software scores every product on those numbers and adds them up. The weights and the full table are on the page, so you can check the result, or download the table to re-rank it for your own priorities.
  • How we check. A separate AI agent that did not build the page recomputes the winners from the raw data and must pass it against our published rules before a new or redesigned page goes live. Routine data refreshes are re-checked by software and go back to that agent if the top 3 change on more than a third of the pages.
  • How often we refresh. Software downloads the data again every 30 days by default and rebuilds and re-checks every page; each page shows its Updated and Next refresh dates.
  • Who is accountable. Our editor, Maximus Milazzo. Corrections: editor@bytherank.com.
Technical details (sources, formulas, quality gate, data files)

The split between AI agents and software

Frontier-model research agents decide what is worth ranking, find and vet datasets, design each ranking's method and audit the results. Deterministic code on our own servers collects the data, computes every metric and score, rebuilds the pages and runs the checks. Every number on a page comes from code applied to public data; routine refreshes are not rewritten by AI.

1. Research: which questions deserve a page

A research agent reviews the questions people search for (search autocomplete suggestions) and checks whether a public, licensed dataset can answer them better than existing pages. A council of independent agents proposes, critiques and scores candidate pages; a page is built only if it states the question it answers, the evidence, the decision it helps with, and a number computed here rather than copied.

2. Sources

U.S. EPA ENERGY STAR certified-product lists (data.energystar.gov), which report each model's values as measured under the applicable U.S. Department of Energy or industry test procedure; U.S. Energy Information Administration electricity prices (Electric Power Monthly). Each page names its dataset, record ids and retrieval date.

3. Formulas

  • Score: each criterion is scored against the best product on the page (best = 100%; lower-is-better values as best divided by value), multiplied by its declared weight; weights sum to 100% (how we rank). A page may instead score each criterion from its worst value on the page (0%) to its best (100%), so that every criterion spreads exactly its weight; such a page says so in its method.
  • Five-year electricity cost (air purifiers): certified annual energy use (kWh/yr) × the EIA U.S. average residential price (date on the page) × 5.
  • Five-year electricity cost (dehumidifiers): computed from the certified capacity and integrated energy factor at DOE's 1,095 dehumidifying hours a year (capacity × 0.4732 ÷ IEF × 1,095 ÷ 24 kWh a year, not the listed yearly figure), × the EIA U.S. average residential price (date on the page) × 5.
  • Duplicate listings: certification lists often carry one machine several times, sometimes under different brands. A model is a certifying partner plus model number; listings with identical certified values on every reported field form one spec set. Ranking pages that merge on every field count spec sets, others count products, and each page states both counts and exactly what was merged.
  • The cut: the list stops above a tie for the last place rather than picking among tied models; if every candidate ties, the page says how many more tie.
  • Why #1 is #1: every ranking shows the #1 vs #2 margin per criterion, the deciding number with its source and date, any equal scores (they share a rank and the page states the order rule) and the smallest single weight change that would change #1, naming the product that would take first place (any product on the page, not only #2), or that none would. All of it is computed from the scores.

4. Quality gate

Before a new or changed set of pages is published, a separate quality agent re-derives the winners and key figures from the raw data and issues a written PASS/FAIL verdict against a published rule set (usefulness, sourcing, no near-duplicate pages, structured data matching visible text, every #1 defensible for the page's question, and a justified order: each page shows why its #1 is #1). New page sets, and a sample of changed ones, also get a blind audit by a further agent on our strongest model. Automated checks refuse any page with fewer than 3 products, a missing value, weights that do not sum to 100%, an undated price or a missing source; a refresh that changes the top 3 on more than a third of the pages goes back to the quality agent.

5. AI disclosure

Introductions and FAQ answers are written with AI models (Anthropic's Claude) from templates filled with the computed numbers. No AI model picks a winner, sets a score or supplies a figure.

6. Open data

Every ranking table is available as CSV and JSON from its page and from all open data. Commissions never change a score (disclosure).
