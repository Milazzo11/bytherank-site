How this ranking is scored

Pollen CADR saturates in the certified data: many models report the same top value, so ranking by it would pick an arbitrary subset. We kept every zero-ozone ENERGY STAR air purifier at that top pollen CADR (450 cfm or more) and ranked the 10 with the highest dust CADR, weighing dust CADR and the five-year electricity cost (certified yearly energy use x current EIA U.S. average residential price x 5).

  • Dust CADR (70%): dust and dust-mite particles; pollen CADR cannot separate these models (higher is better)
  • 5-year electricity cost (30%): the certified yearly energy use priced at the current U.S. average residential electricity rate (EIA) over five years: the running cost you will actually pay (lower is better) Computed: certified kWh per year x EIA U.S. average residential price x 5.
  • Pollen CADR (0%): shown, not scored: every model here has the top certified value (higher is better)

Each number is scored against the best on this page (best = 100%; e.g. 90% of the best capacity = 0.9) and multiplied by its weight; the total is out of 100. Full method.

FAQ

Will an air purifier stop my allergies?

It lowers airborne pollen and dust in the room it runs in; it does not clean bedding or carpets. Ask a doctor about treatment.

Why rank by dust CADR and not pollen CADR?

Every model on this page has a certified pollen CADR of 450 cfm, the top value in the dataset, which 21 zero-ozone models share. Dust CADR still differs: 437 to 586 cfm here.

What does it cost to run one of these?

At the U.S. average residential electricity price of 0.1816 dollars per kWh (EIA, 2026-08), the certified yearly energy use of the models on this page costs 85 to 445 US dollars over five years. Your state's rate may be higher or lower.

Do any of these produce ozone?

No. Every model on this page has zero certified ozone emissions in the ENERGY STAR data.

Where do these numbers come from?

CADR, room size and energy use come from the U.S. EPA ENERGY STAR certified Room Air Cleaners dataset (retrieved 2026-11-01); the electricity price comes from the U.S. EIA Electric Power Monthly (2026-08). We did not test the units ourselves; we rank the certified results and compute the running cost.
