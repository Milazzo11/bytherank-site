How this ranking is scored

We compared the ENERGY STAR whole-home (ducted) dehumidifiers with the highest certified capacity, scoring capacity and IEF. The five-year electricity cost is shown for each (yearly energy computed from certified capacity and IEF at DOE's 1,095 dehumidifying hours a year x current EIA U.S. average residential price x 5; we do not use the listed yearly figure because for some listings it does not match their own capacity and IEF).

  • Certified capacity (45%): water removed per day under the current DOE test (higher is better)
  • Integrated Energy Factor (55%): liters of water removed per kWh including standby power (U.S. DOE test): the efficiency number (higher is better)
  • 5-year electricity cost (0%): shown, not scored (it follows from capacity and IEF, already scored): the yearly energy that certified capacity and IEF imply at DOE's 1,095 dehumidifying hours a year, priced at the current U.S. average residential rate (EIA) over five years; a total, so it grows with capacity (see the cost per 100 pints removed for a like-for-like figure) (lower is better) Computed: certified capacity (pints/day) x 0.473 L per pint / certified IEF (L/kWh) x 1,095 / 24 (DOE's annual dehumidification hours, 10 CFR 430 subpart B appendix X1) x EIA U.S. average residential price x 5.
  • Electricity cost per 100 pints removed (0%): shown, not scored (it follows from IEF): what the electricity costs to remove 100 pints of water at the current U.S. average residential rate (EIA), so units of different capacity compare like for like (lower is better) Computed: 100 pints x 0.473 L per pint / certified IEF (L/kWh) x EIA U.S. average residential price (no operating hours: it depends only on IEF and the rate).

Each number is scored against the best on this page (best = 100%; e.g. 90% of the best capacity = 0.9) and multiplied by its weight; the total is out of 100. Full method.

FAQ

Do I need a whole-house unit or a portable one?

Whole-house units are installed in the HVAC ductwork, usually by a professional; portables plug in and serve one area.

What is IEF?

Integrated Energy Factor: liters of water removed per kilowatt-hour, including standby power, under the U.S. DOE test procedure. Here it runs from 2.22 to 3.26 L/kWh.

What does it cost to run one of these?

At the U.S. average residential electricity price of 0.1816 dollars per kWh (EIA, 2026-08), the yearly energy implied by certified capacity and IEF (1,095 dehumidifying hours a year, DOE) of the models on this page costs 458 to 1,081 US dollars over five years. Your state's rate and how many months it runs change this. The five-year figure is for the water each model removes at those hours, so a larger unit costs more in total; the cost per 100 pints removed compares them like for like, and a smaller unit may run longer in the same damp room.

Where do these numbers come from?

Capacity and IEF come from the U.S. EPA ENERGY STAR certified Dehumidifiers dataset (retrieved 2026-11-01); we compute yearly energy use from them at DOE's 1,095 dehumidifying hours a year, not from the listed yearly figure, and take the electricity price from the U.S. EIA Electric Power Monthly (2026-08). We rank the certified results and compute the running cost; we did not run our own tests.
