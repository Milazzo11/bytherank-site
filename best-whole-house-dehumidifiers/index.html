How this ranking is scored

We compared the 10 ENERGY STAR whole-home (ducted) dehumidifiers with the highest certified capacity, scoring capacity and IEF. The five-year electricity cost is shown for each (certified yearly energy use x current EIA U.S. average residential price x 5).

  • Certified capacity (45%): water removed per day under the current DOE test (higher is better)
  • Integrated Energy Factor (55%): liters of water removed per kWh including standby power (U.S. DOE test): the efficiency number (higher is better)
  • 5-year electricity cost (0%): shown, not scored (it follows from capacity and IEF, already scored): the certified yearly energy use priced at the current U.S. average residential rate (EIA) over five years (lower is better) Computed: certified kWh per year x EIA U.S. average residential price x 5.

Each number is scored against the best on this page (best = 100%; e.g. 90% of the best capacity = 0.9) and multiplied by its weight; the total is out of 100. Full method.

FAQ

Do I need a whole-house unit or a portable one?

Whole-house units are installed in the HVAC ductwork, usually by a professional; portables plug in and serve one area.

What is IEF?

Integrated Energy Factor: liters of water removed per kilowatt-hour, including standby power, under the U.S. DOE test procedure. Here it runs from 2.22 to 3.26 L/kWh.

What does it cost to run one of these?

At the U.S. average residential electricity price of 0.1816 dollars per kWh (EIA, 2026-08), the certified yearly energy use of the models on this page costs 380 to 2,550 US dollars over five years. Your state's rate and how many months it runs change this.

Where do these numbers come from?

Capacity, IEF and yearly energy use come from the U.S. EPA ENERGY STAR certified Dehumidifiers dataset (retrieved 2026-11-01); the electricity price from the U.S. EIA Electric Power Monthly (2026-08). We rank the certified results and compute the running cost; we did not run our own tests.
