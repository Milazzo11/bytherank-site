How this ranking is scored

We compared the zero-ozone ENERGY STAR certified air purifiers with the highest certified smoke CADR. Smoke CADR carries 85% of the weight and the five-year electricity cost 15% (cost is scored against the page's cheapest unit, so a larger share would let a slow but cheap unit lead), computed from the certified yearly energy use and the current EIA U.S. average residential price. Rated room size is left out of the score because ENERGY STAR derives it from smoke CADR.

  • Smoke CADR (85%): how fast the unit removes smoke-sized particles, the smallest of the three certified test particles (higher is better)
  • 5-year electricity cost (15%): the certified yearly energy use priced at the current U.S. average residential electricity rate (EIA) over five years; the certified yearly energy use assumes about 16 hours of running a day (the certified records imply a median of 5,904 hours a year), so running it around the clock costs about 1.5 times the figure shown (lower is better) Computed: certified kWh per year x EIA U.S. average residential price x 5.

Each number is scored against the best on this page (best = 100%; e.g. 90% of the best capacity = 0.9) and multiplied by its weight; the total is out of 100. Full method.

FAQ

How much smoke CADR do the models here have?

From 376 to 577 cfm, the highest values among 124 zero-ozone certified spec sets, except one with no listing we could confirm and two listed by a store but not in stock or with no printed price, which are named under the table, not ranked.

What does it cost to run one of these?

At the U.S. average residential electricity price of 0.1816 dollars per kWh (EIA, 2026-08), the certified yearly energy use of the models on this page costs 213 to 445 US dollars over five years. Your state's rate may be higher or lower.

Why only zero-ozone models?

Ozone irritates the lungs (U.S. EPA). Each reports 0 ppb ozone in its ENERGY STAR certification, as its maker reported it. UL's zero-ozone test (UL 2998) cannot quantify a reading below 5 ppb, so 0 ppb and the small non-zero reports some other models carry are not reliably different; keeping only 0 ppb reports is our conservative choice, and the units it leaves out that would score highest are named under the table (see also our air purifier ozone and room-fit page).

Does smoke CADR cover smoke smell and gases?

No. CADR measures how fast a purifier removes particles (smoke, dust and pollen particles in the certified test). Smoke smell and gases are removed by activated carbon or other gas filters, which the ENERGY STAR certification does not test, so this page does not rank them; check the maker's carbon filter details for odor.

Where do these numbers come from?

CADR, room size and energy use come from the U.S. EPA ENERGY STAR certified Room Air Cleaners dataset (retrieved 2026-11-01); the electricity price comes from the U.S. EIA Electric Power Monthly (2026-08). We did not test the units ourselves; we rank the certified results and compute the running cost.

Is noise included?

No. The certification data does not include noise, so check the maker's decibel rating if the unit will run in a bedroom.
