How this ranking is scored

We compared the 10 zero-ozone ENERGY STAR certified air purifiers with the highest certified smoke CADR. Smoke is the hardest of the three CADR tests (smoke, dust, pollen) because the particles are the smallest, so it gets the largest weight.

  • Smoke CADR (60%): how fast the unit removes smoke-sized particles, the hardest test (higher is better)
  • Smoke CADR per watt (25%): clean air per watt; matters when it runs all day during smoke events (higher is better)
  • Rated room size (15%): the largest room it is certified to clean (higher is better)

Each number is scored against the best on this page (best = 100%; e.g. 90% of the best capacity = 0.9) and multiplied by its weight; the total is out of 100. Full method.

FAQ

What smoke CADR do I need?

A common rule of thumb (AHAM) is a smoke CADR of at least two thirds of the room's floor area: about 200 cfm for a 300 sq ft room. The models here range from 413 to 577 cfm.

Why only zero-ozone models?

Ozone is a lung irritant. Every model on this page has 0.00 ppb certified ozone emissions in the ENERGY STAR data.

Where do these numbers come from?

From the U.S. EPA ENERGY STAR certified product dataset, which lists the CADR and energy results each model was certified with. We did not test the units ourselves; we rank the certified results.

Does a higher CADR mean more noise?

Usually, at the top fan speed. The certification data does not include noise, so check the maker's decibel rating if the unit will run in a bedroom.
