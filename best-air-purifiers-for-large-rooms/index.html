How this ranking is scored

We kept zero-ozone ENERGY STAR air purifiers certified for rooms of 500 sq ft or more and took those with the lowest five-year electricity cost (certified yearly energy use x current EIA U.S. average residential price x 5). Running cost carries most of the weight, rated room size the rest. Every model tied with the last one on the selection criterion is listed, so the cut never picks among equals.

  • 5-year electricity cost (60%): the certified yearly energy use priced at the current U.S. average residential electricity rate (EIA) over five years: the running cost you will actually pay (lower is better) Computed: certified kWh per year x EIA U.S. average residential price x 5.
  • Rated room size (40%): the largest room ENERGY STAR certifies it for (it follows smoke CADR, so it stands in for cleaning speed) (higher is better)

Each number is scored against the best on this page (best = 100%; e.g. 90% of the best capacity = 0.9) and multiplied by its weight; the total is out of 100. Full method.

FAQ

How much does a bigger room rating cost to run?

On this page, rated room size runs from 502 to 894 sq ft and five-year electricity cost from 74 to 310 US dollars at the U.S. average price (2026-08).

Do any of these produce ozone?

No. Every model on this page has zero certified ozone emissions in the ENERGY STAR data.

Where do these numbers come from?

CADR, room size and energy use come from the U.S. EPA ENERGY STAR certified Room Air Cleaners dataset (retrieved 2026-11-01); the electricity price comes from the U.S. EIA Electric Power Monthly (2026-08). We did not test the units ourselves; we rank the certified results and compute the running cost.
