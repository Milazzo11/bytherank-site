How this ranking is scored

We compared the zero-ozone ENERGY STAR air purifiers with the highest certified smoke CADR per watt, weighing efficiency, smoke CADR and the five-year electricity cost (certified yearly energy use x current EIA U.S. average residential price x 5). The CleanAirKits Luggable Ultra (5-fan) (certified as Rainbow Luggable Ultra, record 4518200) is left out: its certified yearly energy and its CADR per watt disagree, so one of the two printed numbers would be wrong. Change on 2026-11-04: we found the maker's Amazon listing for the DREAME CVA10B and added it. Its five-year electricity cost is the lowest on this page and each criterion scores against the page's best, so the other models' cost points fell: the CleanAirKits Luggable XL Ultra (7-fan) stays #1, but its lead over #2 narrowed from 3.2 to 1.5 points, the deciding number moved from electricity cost to CADR per watt, and the LEVOIT LAP-C311S-WUSR left the top 10.

  • Smoke CADR per watt (60%): smoke-free clean air per watt, a field in each ENERGY STAR certification record; ENERGY STAR's Version 3.0 qualifying metric is a different one (the Integrated Energy Factor, PM2.5 CADR per watt including standby), so this page's ranking is not ENERGY STAR's efficiency score (higher is better)
  • Smoke CADR (20%): how fast the unit removes smoke-sized particles, the smallest of the three certified test particles (higher is better)
  • 5-year electricity cost (20%): the certified yearly energy use priced at the current U.S. average residential electricity rate (EIA) over five years; the certified yearly energy use assumes about 16 hours of running a day (the certified records imply a median of 5,904 hours a year), so running it around the clock costs about 1.5 times the figure shown (lower is better) Computed: certified kWh per year x EIA U.S. average residential price x 5.

Each number is scored against the best on this page (best = 100%; e.g. 90% of the best capacity = 0.9) and multiplied by its weight; the total is out of 100. Full method.

FAQ

What does CADR per watt mean?

Clean air delivery rate (cubic feet per minute of smoke-free air) divided by the power the unit draws, as listed in each ENERGY STAR certification record. ENERGY STAR's own qualifying metric is the Integrated Energy Factor (PM2.5 CADR per watt), a separate field.

What does it cost to run one of these?

At the U.S. average residential electricity price of 0.1816 dollars per kWh (EIA, 2026-08), the certified yearly energy use of the models on this page costs 29 to 369 US dollars over five years. Your state's rate may be higher or lower.

Do any of these produce ozone?

Each reports 0 ppb ozone in its ENERGY STAR certification, as its maker reported it. UL's zero-ozone test (UL 2998) cannot quantify a reading below 5 ppb, so 0 ppb and the small non-zero reports some other models carry are not reliably different; keeping only 0 ppb reports is our conservative choice, and the units it leaves out that would score highest are named under the table (see also our air purifier ozone and room-fit page).

Where do these numbers come from?

CADR, room size and energy use come from the U.S. EPA ENERGY STAR certified Room Air Cleaners dataset (retrieved 2026-11-01); the electricity price comes from the U.S. EIA Electric Power Monthly (2026-08). We did not test the units ourselves; we rank the certified results and compute the running cost.
