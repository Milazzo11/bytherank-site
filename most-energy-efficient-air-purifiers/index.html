How this ranking is scored

We compared the 10 zero-ozone ENERGY STAR air purifiers with the highest certified smoke CADR per watt, weighing efficiency, smoke CADR and the five-year electricity cost (certified yearly energy use x current EIA U.S. average residential price x 5).

  • Smoke CADR per watt (60%): clean air per watt, the efficiency figure ENERGY STAR certifies (higher is better)
  • Smoke CADR (20%): how fast the unit removes smoke-sized particles, the smallest of the three certified test particles (higher is better)
  • 5-year electricity cost (20%): the certified yearly energy use priced at the current U.S. average residential electricity rate (EIA) over five years: the running cost you will actually pay (lower is better) Computed: certified kWh per year x EIA U.S. average residential price x 5.

Each number is scored against the best on this page (best = 100%; e.g. 90% of the best capacity = 0.9) and multiplied by its weight; the total is out of 100. Full method.

FAQ

What does CADR per watt mean?

Clean air delivery rate (cubic feet per minute of filtered air) divided by the power the unit draws. ENERGY STAR certifies it per model.

What does it cost to run one of these?

At the U.S. average residential electricity price of 0.1816 dollars per kWh (EIA, 2026-08), the certified yearly energy use of the models on this page costs 29 to 304 US dollars over five years. Your state's rate may be higher or lower.

Do any of these produce ozone?

No. Every model on this page has zero certified ozone emissions in the ENERGY STAR data.

Where do these numbers come from?

CADR, room size and energy use come from the U.S. EPA ENERGY STAR certified Room Air Cleaners dataset (retrieved 2026-11-01); the electricity price comes from the U.S. EIA Electric Power Monthly (2026-08). We did not test the units ourselves; we rank the certified results and compute the running cost.
